Hubness explained by closeness

Take two points, c (red) and x (black), and the ball around c whose radius reaches x. The ball's probability mass is the closeness of x to c. A point is a hub when it is close to few points, so it is picked as a neighbour often, and an anti-hub when it is picked rarely.

Here C = C(x;c), Bc(rxc) is the ball centred at c with radius rxc = ‖x − c‖, and fX is the density. In the explorer D = 2 and fX is uniform on [−1, 1]², so fX = 1/4.

Hubness explorer uniform ball in D dimensions · x = origin

By rotational symmetry the closeness of the origin to c depends only on s = ‖c‖. So the expectation over c reduces to one integral in s, with radial density D sD−1:

Ok:N(0)=N∫01Nk:N(s)DsD−1ds,s=‖c‖

The closeness of the origin is sD for s ≤ ½. For larger s it is the lens where the ball around c meets the unit ball, divided by the unit-ball volume, computed with the regularised incomplete beta function. N and k come from the sidebar.
